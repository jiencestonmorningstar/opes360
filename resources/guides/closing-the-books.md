# Closing the books

A financial year is divided into periods — normally months. Closing a period
means the books for that month are final: nothing new can be posted into it.

## Why close anything

So that "what did the books say in March" keeps having one answer. Without
closing, a payment recorded late, or an invoice dated wrongly, silently
changes a month you already reported on — and you find out when the figures
you gave your accountant no longer match the figures in the system.

## Setting up your year

**Accounting → Periods.** Create a financial year with its start date, and
twelve monthly periods are created with it.

Your financial year does **not** have to start in January. If yours runs July
to June, set it to start on 1 July and the periods follow.

## Closing and reopening

Close a period once you have finished with that month. From then on, any
attempt to post into it — an invoice dated back into it, a payment recorded
late — is refused, and whoever tried is told why.

**Closing is reversible.** A period closed on the wrong day is a real thing
that happens, and a product that made it permanent would just teach people
never to close anything. Reopen it, fix what needs fixing, close it again.
Who closed it and when is recorded either way.

Closing a whole year closes every month inside it. Reopening a year does
**not** reopen its months — reopening the year is permission to reopen a
month; it is not a decision that all twelve should be open again.

## If you never set any of this up

Nothing changes. A business that has not defined any periods can post to any
date, exactly as before. This is entirely opt-in.

## Cost centres

A cost centre answers "which budget does this come out of" — separate from a
department, which answers "who does this belong to". One department can run
two separately funded projects, and a business usually wants to see both
splits.

## Cash flow

**Accounting → Cash flow** shows what actually moved through your cash and
bank accounts over a period, with an opening and closing balance.

It reads the movements themselves rather than working backwards from profit,
which means you can check it line by line against your own bank statement.
Entries from sales and purchases are shown as operating; anything posted
through "other operations" is shown as **unclassified** rather than guessed
at — a statement that sorted those into investing or financing by assumption
would look authoritative and be wrong.
