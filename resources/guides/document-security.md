# Who can see a document

Every document carries a **security level**. It decides who can open it, and it
is enforced by the product itself — not merely by leaving the document out of a
list.

## The four levels

| Level | Who can open it |
|---|---|
| **Public** | Anybody who can see documents at all |
| **Internal** | The same. This is the default |
| **Confidential** | The same — but the document is visibly marked |
| **Restricted** | Only the person who owns it, and holders of document administration |

## Confidential marks; restricted closes

This is the distinction worth understanding, because the names sound
interchangeable and are not.

**Confidential** is a marking. It tells whoever opens the document to treat it
carefully — it shows a badge, it can carry a watermark — but your colleagues can
still read it. That is the point: a business has to be able to work on its
sensitive documents.

**Restricted** actually narrows the audience. A restricted document is visible
only to the person it belongs to and to whoever administers documents. Everybody
else cannot open it, cannot find it, and cannot reach it by its link.

If every sensitive document were restricted, people would simply stop marking
documents at all, and the marking would become worthless. So only the top level
closes the door.

## Owning a document

Every document can have an **owner** — the person responsible for it, not
necessarily whoever created it. For restricted documents this matters directly:
the owner is the one person besides the document administrators who can open it.

Set the owner when you file a document, or change it later. Changing the owner
of a signed, issued document is allowed and does not invalidate it: filing is
not editing.

## Sharing

Sending a document outside the business is a separate permission from reading
it. Somebody who can open every document in the company may still not be able to
share one.

This is not bureaucracy. Whoever can share can put a signed contract in front of
somebody who was never meant to see it, and no amount of read access should
imply that.

## Things that surprise people

**Restricted really means restricted, even for senior people.** A Manager with
full document access still cannot open a restricted document that belongs to
somebody else. Only the owner and document administrators can — and by default
that means the Owner and Administrators of the business.

**The business Owner is never locked out.** Whoever owns the account can always
reach their own business's documents.

**Filing a document never breaks its signature.** Moving an issued document into
a folder, tagging it, assigning an owner or recording an expiry date all leave
its tamper-proof seal intact. Organising a document is not the same as changing
it, and the product treats them differently.

**Hiding is not the same as refusing.** A document you are not allowed to see is
refused at the source — by URL, by search, by link, by the API. It is not merely
absent from a list.
