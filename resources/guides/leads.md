# Leads

An enquiry that is not yet a deal: somebody rang, walked in, or filled the
contact form, and might become a customer. Find them under **Leads**, beside
the pipeline.

## The funnel

New → working → qualified → converted or lost. Moving between the middle
stages is just dragging the work along. The two ends are different:

- **Converting** creates a real customer in your book — and a deal, if you
  give it a value — and links the lead to what it became. The lead is not
  deleted; it is the record of where the customer came from.
- **Losing** requires a reason, and keeps it. "Why do we lose work" is the
  most useful question this register can answer, and it has no answer if
  every lost enquiry simply disappears.

A converted or lost lead cannot be dragged back — those states carry side
effects. Raise a fresh lead if they come back.

## Activities

Calls, meetings, notes and tasks, against a lead or a deal. An activity with
a due date and no done date is overdue, and **deals nobody has touched in a
fortnight** is the single most valuable list here — work is rarely lost by a
no, it is lost by silence.

## The forecast

The forecast weights each open deal's value by how likely its stage is to
close, and buckets by expected close month. A deal past its date rolls into
the current month rather than flattering a past one; a deal with no date is
shown as unscheduled rather than guessed.

Each stage carries a default probability; a deal can override it when you
know better. The numbers are read from the pipeline — there is nothing to
maintain and nothing to disagree with it.

## Who can do what

Leads use the pipeline's own abilities (`deals.view`, `deals.create`, …).
Chasing an enquiry and chasing a deal are the same job in the businesses this
product serves, so there is no separate permission to administer.
