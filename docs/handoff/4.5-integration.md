# Phase 4.5 — Audit trail & governance

## 1. What the audit trail captured before this phase

`activity_log` and `AuditObserver` have existed since the first release. The
inventory below is what was actually there on inspection, not what the name
suggests.

**Observed models — 9, registered in `AppServiceProvider::boot()`:**
`Company`, `User`, `Contact`, `Item`, `Document`, `Payment`, `Receipt`,
`Artisan`, `BusinessDocument`.

**Events captured:** `created`, `updated`, `trashed`, `deleted`. Nothing else.

**Fields recorded:** `company_id`, `user_id` (deliberately `auth('web')`),
`event`, `subject_type`, `subject_id`, `properties` (attribute-level
before/after diff on update, full sanitised attributes on create), `ip`,
`user_agent`, `created_at`. Secrets are redacted by an allow-list.

**Indexes:** `(company_id, created_at)` and `(subject_type, subject_id)`.

### The gaps found

| Gap | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **No screen at all.** Nothing in `routes/web.php`, no Livewire component | The rows existed and nobody could look at them. `DocumentActivity` reads the table for one document type; that is the only reader in the codebase. The trail was worthless in the exact dispute it is kept for |
| **No reads logged.** Not one | Nothing records that a manager opened forty payslips the week before resigning |
| **No index on `user_id`** | "What has this person been doing?" — the first question anyone asks — was a full scan of the company's history |
| **No `subject_label`** | An entry for a deleted record resolved to nothing. The screen could only show a ULID for exactly the rows that matter most |
| **No `restored` event** | The trail showed a deletion that apparently never ended |
| **No permission-change audit** | A grant lives in the `company_user_permission` pivot, which no observed model loads. Someone could be given `payables.execute` and the trail would be silent |
| **No export audit** | Data leaving the system entirely was the one act with no record |
| **No governance view** | `Permissions.php` argues every separation of duty in careful comments; nothing ever checked whether the live grants still honoured them |
| **~30 unobserved models touching money, permissions or people** | See §5 |

The rule was followed: nothing here is a second audit log. Everything writes to
`activity_log` through one class.

## 3. Sensitive-access logging — where the line is drawn, and why

A read is logged when **one** of these is true:

1. The record is **one named person's confidential business** — a payslip, an
   employment contract, a performance review.
2. The business has **explicitly marked the document restricted** —
   `BusinessDocument` at `security = restricted` or `confidential`, opened by
   somebody who is not its owner.
3. The read **takes data out of the system** — any export or bundle download.

Everything else is not logged: invoices, customers, stock, deals, every list
screen. The defence of that line is arithmetic. A ten-person business does
maybe 200 writes a day; the same business does tens of thousands of reads. Log
them all and the writes — the only thing anyone has ever needed to prove — are
one row in five hundred, and the table doubles weekly. A log nobody can search
is not a control, it is a storage bill.

**The window.** `Audit::accessed()` writes at most one row per person per record
per 15 minutes (`Audit::READ_WINDOW_MINUTES`), through an atomic `Cache::add`
so two tabs cannot race. "Awa looked at Marie's pay this afternoon" is the fact
a dispute needs; "Awa looked four times" has never been asked. Exports are
deliberately **not** windowed — two exports are two spreadsheets on two laptops,
and collapsing them hides the second copy, which is the one that matters.

**Contents are never copied in.** Recording the net pay next to "this payslip
was read" would double the number of places a salary is readable, which is the
opposite of the point. Asserted by test.

### Call sites the orchestrator must add (I do not own these files)

The service and its policy are built and tested; three one-line calls activate
it. All are additive, none change behaviour:

```php
// app/Livewire/Payroll/Show.php — in mount(), after the Gate::authorize
Audit::accessed($payslip, 'Opened a payslip');

// app/Livewire/Papers/Show.php — in mount(), only for closed documents
if ($document->isRestricted() && $document->owner_id !== auth()->id()) {
    Audit::accessed($document, 'Opened a restricted document');
}

// app/Services/Documents/DocumentBundles.php — where the ZIP is built
Audit::exported('document bundle', $documents->count(), ['package' => $package->id]);
```

Plus, wherever a role or per-user override is written (`app/Livewire/Team/Show.php`
and the invitation flow):

```php
Audit::permissionChanged($user, "Given payables.execute in {$company->name}");
```

That last one is the biggest remaining hole in the trail and I would prioritise
it over any of the reads: a permission grant is the act that makes every other
act possible, and today it leaves no trace at all.

## 4. Permission keys — and why audit access is gated the way it is

Two new keys, **not** added to `Permissions::CATALOGUE` by me (that file is off
limits). `AuditServiceProvider` defines them and steps aside the moment they are
catalogued, so adding them is a pure addition with no file to delete.

| Key | Grants |
|---|---|
| `audit.view` | The audit screen — the whole business's history |
| `audit.govern` | The governance screen — the permission matrix and conflict report |

**Proposed catalogue entry:**

```php
/*
 * The trail, and the map of who can do what.
 *
 * `view` is not implied by anything and is granted by hand, because it is
 * strictly more powerful than any single module's own `view`: the before/after
 * diffs let somebody denied `payroll.view` reconstruct a good deal of the
 * payroll anyway. `govern` is separate again — the conflict report is a map of
 * where this business's controls are weakest, which is the single most useful
 * document to anyone planning to abuse them.
 */
'Audit' => ['view', 'govern'],
```

**Seeder:** Owner and Administrator only. Not the Manager, not the Accountant.
The people an audit log constrains must not be the people who can read it and
work out what it noticed. (An `auditor` role that holds `audit.view` and
nothing else is the obvious next step, and the reason `view` and `govern` are
split rather than one key.)

Until the catalogue entry lands, `hasPermissionIn` returns false for every
non-Owner, so the screens are Owner-only. That is a safe default and the tests
assert a Manager is refused.

**Routes to add** (both need `can:` middleware as well as the in-component
`Gate::authorize`, per the codebase convention):

```php
Route::get('/audit', \App\Livewire\Audit\Index::class)
    ->middleware('can:audit.view')->name('audit');
Route::get('/audit/governance', \App\Livewire\Audit\Governance::class)
    ->middleware('can:audit.govern')->name('audit.governance');
```

## 5. Models needing observer registration in `AppServiceProvider`

`AppServiceProvider` is off limits to me. Add these to the existing
`foreach ([...] as $model)` list. The criterion applied throughout: **money,
permissions, or a person's record** — and the record must be *mutable*, because
an append-only ledger already is its own audit trail and observing it would bury
the signal (the same reasoning the existing comment gives for excluding
`DocumentLine` and `StockMovement`).

**Tier 1 — money that moves, and the decisions that release it. Register these first.**

```php
Payslip::class, PayrollRun::class,
PaymentRun::class, PaymentRunItem::class,
Expense::class, ExpensePayment::class,
ExpenseClaim::class, ExpenseClaimReimbursement::class,
Refund::class,
BankAccount::class, AccountTransfer::class,
```

**Tier 2 — the books themselves, where a restatement is invisible after the fact.**

```php
JournalEntry::class, LedgerAccount::class, TaxRate::class, FiscalPeriod::class,
```

`JournalLine` is deliberately excluded: an entry is balanced and immutable once
posted, so the header carries the story and the lines would triple the rows.

**Tier 3 — permissions and the controls over them. Arguably tier 1.**

```php
Role::class, CompanyUserPermission::class,
Workflow::class, WorkflowStep::class, WorkflowDecision::class,
WebhookEndpoint::class,
```

`CompanyUserPermission` is the important one and the current blind spot — see
§3. `WorkflowStep` matters because `Permissions.php` says editing a workflow is
the same as being able to spend the money.

**Tier 4 — a person's record. Everything here is somebody's livelihood or reputation.**

```php
Employee::class, EmploymentContract::class, SalaryComponent::class,
LeaveRequest::class, PerformanceReview::class, Position::class, Department::class,
```

**Tier 5 — commitments and custody.**

```php
FixedAsset::class, AssetTransfer::class,
PurchaseRequisition::class, Rfq::class, RfqSupplier::class,
Deal::class, Project::class,
BusinessDocumentShare::class, Device::class,
```

**Deliberately NOT observed**, with reasons, so this is a decision rather than
an oversight: `StockMovement`, `DocumentLine`, `JournalLine`,
`PaymentAllocation`, `DepreciationEntry`, `LoyaltyTransaction` (all append-only
or immutable — already their own trail); `ActivityLog` (recursion);
`WebhookDelivery`, `SyncReceipt`, `IdempotencyKey`, `NumberLease` (machine
plumbing, already logged elsewhere); `Notification*` (volume, no dispute value);
`FormResponse`, `VerificationScan` (append-only by nature).

**Cost warning worth taking seriously:** each registration is one extra INSERT
per write. Registering all ~30 roughly triples `activity_log`'s growth. Tier 1–3
is where nearly all the value is; tiers 4–5 could reasonably wait until there is
a retention policy for the table, which there currently is not — **and that is
the largest thing this phase does not solve.** `activity_log` has no pruning, no
partitioning and no archive. See §7.

## 6. `config/modules.php` placement — audit should not be switchable

**Recommendation: do not add audit to `config/modules.php` at all.** If it must
appear for catalogue completeness, it goes in with `'switchable' => false`,
alongside `partners`.

The argument: `Modules::forAbility()` is consulted in `Gate::before`, and a
module that is off denies its abilities outright. So making audit switchable
means a business can switch off its own audit trail. Every reason someone would
want that is a reason they should not be allowed to.

It is worse than it first looks. The person with the motive to switch it off is
the person about to do something they do not want recorded, and they are
plausibly the Administrator who holds `settings.update`. A control that its
subject can disable is not a control. It is also not the business's own choice
in the way a module is — an audit trail exists partly for third parties: a tax
inspector, a bank, a court, an insurer. "We turned it off" is not an answer any
of them accept.

The capture side is unswitchable regardless — `AuditObserver` is registered in
`AppServiceProvider` and does not consult `Modules`. The only thing a module
toggle could disable is *reading* the trail, which is precisely backwards.

The counter-argument I considered and rejected: a one-person business does not
want an audit menu item, and modules are how this codebase hides irrelevant
screens. That is a navigation problem, not a permissions one — and it already
solves itself, since only Owners and Administrators will hold `audit.view`.

## 7. Design decisions worth challenging

1. **`History` is not gated on `audit.view`.** Deliberate. That ability is sight
   of the whole business's history; requiring it would mean nobody but an
   auditor ever sees a document's own timeline and the panel would be dead code.
   The gate that matters is the one the host page already applied to reach the
   record. What the panel enforces itself is the tenant boundary — its
   parameters arrive from the browser and `ActivityLog` has no `CompanyScope`,
   so a guessed ULID would otherwise return another business's before/after
   values. Tested. **If you disagree, the fix is a `subjectType`-driven policy
   check in `History::mount()`, not adding `audit.view`.**

2. **The company filter is hand-written on every query.** `ActivityLog`
   deliberately has no `CompanyScope` (it records system-level events with no
   company). That means there is no safety net: drop the `where('company_id')`
   line in `Index` or `History` and it silently leaks across tenants. Both
   filter first and unconditionally, and a null company yields no rows rather
   than all rows. This is the most fragile thing in the phase and the first
   place to look in review.

3. **The Owner is excluded from the conflict report, not flagged.**
   `hasPermissionIn` short-circuits true for Owners, so they trip all 13 rules
   and would drown every actionable finding. They are listed separately with an
   explanation instead. Challengeable: an auditor might argue the Owner *is* the
   finding in a one-person finance team. `SegregationOfDuties::unavoidable()`
   returns exactly that list if you want it promoted.

4. **A real finding in the shipped defaults, asserted rather than fixed:** the
   seeded **Manager role holds both `expenses.create` and `expenses.pay`** — one
   person can enter what a supplier is owed and settle it. `RolePermissionSeeder`
   is off limits to me and this may well be the right trade for a small
   business, but it should be a decision somebody takes rather than one nobody
   noticed. `SegregationOfDutiesTest::test_the_seeded_manager_can_both_enter_a_supplier_bill_and_pay_it`
   locks in the current answer either way. The payables `approve`/`execute`
   split the seeder comments promise **is** correctly honoured — also asserted.

5. **Conflict rules are validated against the live catalogue by a test.** A rule
   naming a renamed ability can never match, so the report would say "no
   problems" forever — failing open, the worst failure mode for a control
   report. `test_every_conflict_rule_names_permissions_that_actually_exist`
   makes that a red build instead. Any new module adding money abilities should
   add a rule here.

6. **No retention policy.** `activity_log` grows forever, and §5 roughly triples
   the rate. This is the largest thing left undone. The awkward part is that
   pruning an audit log is itself a governance decision — a business that
   deletes last year's trail has destroyed evidence — so it needs a stated
   retention period per company and a record of the pruning, not a cron job that
   quietly deletes. `DocumentRetention` is the pattern to copy.

7. **Read logging depends on the cache driver.** The 15-minute window uses
   `Cache::add`. On the `array` driver (or a cache flush) the window resets and
   duplicate rows appear. That degrades to "slightly noisier", never to "missing
   entries", which is the right direction — but a production deployment should
   have a shared cache store or the window is per-process.

8. **`subject_label` is a snapshot, not a link.** It says what the record was
   called *at that moment*. A customer renamed after the fact shows its old name
   in old entries. That is correct for an audit trail and will look like a bug
   to somebody once.
