# Phase 4.8 — Fleet management

## What fleet actually needed, honestly

Less than expected.

A vehicle is a `FixedAsset`. Before writing a line of this phase I went through
what a fleet feature is normally asked to do and checked it against what asset
locations, custodians, transfers and maintenance already do. The overlap is
almost total:

| Fleet asks for | Already answered by |
|---|---|
| A vehicle register | `fixed_assets` — with cost, depreciation and disposal thrown in |
| Where the vehicle is kept | `asset_locations` + `fixed_assets.asset_location_id` |
| Who the driver is | `fixed_assets.custodian_id` |
| Reassigning a vehicle | `AssetMovements::transfer()`, with the history kept |
| Vehicle history | `asset_transfers` |
| Service schedule | `asset_maintenance`, including work repeating every N months |
| Marking a service done | `AssetServicing::complete()` |
| What a service cost | The `Expense` the maintenance record points at |
| Vehicle disposal | `AssetRegister` disposal, already there |

So the phase came down to five things assets genuinely do not have:

1. **Vehicle identity papers** — plate, VIN, make/model/year, fuel type, tank
   size, and the three expiry dates. A generator carries none of these.
2. **A milometer.** This is the real gap. The register has no concept of *use*
   at all — a generator is worth what it is worth whether it ran or not.
3. **Fuel.** Litres and a reading at the pump; consumption falls out of those.
4. **Servicing on distance instead of on the calendar.** The single most
   valuable extension, and the only one that touches existing code.
5. **Expiry alerts** for insurance, roadworthiness and licence.

Everything else was already built. Nothing here creates a second vehicle
registry, a second driver assignment, a second location concept, a second
scheduler, or a second copy of a fuel cost.

## What shipped

### New tables (`2026_09_11_000801_create_fleet_tables.php`)

- **`vehicle_details`** — one-to-one on `fixed_asset_id` (unique, cascade).
  **Why an extension and not a root entity:** a `vehicles` table would fork the
  van's identity. Its cost would live on the asset and its plate on the
  vehicle, with nothing forcing the two rows to describe the same van, and
  every asset feature written since — depreciation, disposal, transfers,
  servicing, documents, departments — would need a second implementation
  pointed at the second table. The one-to-one costs a join; the alternative
  costs the whole register.
- **`vehicle_trips`** — date, driver, start/end odometer, purpose. No
  `distance` column: it is `end - start`, and a stored third copy would
  disagree with the two readings the moment anybody corrected one.
- **`fuel_logs`** — litres, odometer at fill, full-tank flag, `expense_id`.
  **No `cost` column at all.** `AssetMaintenance` caches a `cost` read off the
  expense; fuel deliberately does not even do that, and `FuelLog::cost()` reads
  through the relation each time. There is exactly one copy of the figure.

### Extension to `asset_maintenance` (`..._000802_...`)

Three nullable columns, no new table: `due_at_odometer`,
`completed_at_odometer`, `interval_km`. A van serviced every 10 000 km and a
generator serviced every six months are the same commitment on different
clocks. A parallel scheduler would produce two outstanding lists, two overdue
counts, and an argument about which is right.

The index is named `asset_maint_company_odo_idx` explicitly — `asset_maintenance`
is already a long prefix and the generated name would be a MySQL 64-character
risk that SQLite would accept silently.

### Models

- `App\Models\VehicleDetail`, `VehicleTrip`, `FuelLog` — all `BelongsToCompany`.
- `FixedAsset::vehicle()`, `trips()`, `fuelLogs()`, `isVehicle()` (additive).
  Named `vehicle`, **not** `registration` or `details` — the shadowing trap that
  gave `locationRecord` its name. There is a `registration` column on the other
  side and a relation of that name would be silently unreachable.
- `AssetMaintenance::isDueAtDistance(?int)` and `isScheduledByDistance()`.
  **`isOverdue()` is untouched.** Distance-due is a *separate* method because
  the odometer is not on the row — it is derived — so answering needs a reading
  passed in. Folding it into `isOverdue()` would have meant a method that
  quietly returned false whenever nobody had a reading.

### Services

- `App\Services\Fleet\VehicleUsage` — `odometer()`, `logTrip()`, `logFuel()`,
  `consumptionPer100Km()`, `distanceTravelled()`.
- `App\Services\Fleet\FleetAlerts` — `expiringPapers()`, `servicingDue()`.
- `AssetServicing::complete()` gained a fourth, optional `?int $odometer`
  parameter and now raises the next visit when `interval_months` **or**
  `interval_km` is set. Existing behaviour is unchanged; all of
  `tests/Feature/AssetMovementsTest.php` still passes.

### Screen

`App\Livewire\Fleet\Vehicles` + `resources/views/livewire/fleet/vehicles.blade.php`
(with a `partials/details-form.blade.php`). Four tabs: the fleet, trips, fuel,
papers. Links across to Movements; Movements is where you still transfer a van
and hand it to a driver, because that has not changed.

## Design decisions worth challenging

**The odometer is derived, never stored.** `VehicleUsage::odometer()` is the
highest of `vehicle_trips.end_odometer`, `fuel_logs.odometer` and
`asset_maintenance.completed_at_odometer`. A stored counter goes stale the first
time somebody corrects a trip, and then the servicing schedule counts from a
number nobody can reproduce. The cost is three `MAX` queries per vehicle per
render; the screen memoises per vehicle. **Challenge this if a fleet ever gets
large enough that it matters** — the fix is a cached column with the derivation
as its authority, not the other way round.

**No reading means no reading, not zero.** `odometer()` returns `null` when
nothing has been recorded. A van reading 0 is brand new; a van reading nothing
is unmeasured, and conflating them would schedule a 10 000 km service off a
fictional starting point.

**Completing a `interval_km` job without a reading throws.** "Every 10 000 km"
from an unknown starting point is a guess, not a schedule, and a job raised at
an indefensible reading sits either permanently overdue or permanently
invisible. The reading is inferred from trips and fills first, so this only
fires when literally nothing is known. **This is the most arguable call in the
phase** — the alternative is to complete quietly and drop the repeat, which
loses a standing commitment without saying so.

**Consumption excludes the first fill's litres.** Tank-to-tank measurement:
distance between the first and last reading, against the fuel put in *after* the
first reading. Including the first tankful flatters the figure by roughly one
tank, which on a light van is most of the answer.

**A distance-only repeat gets no `due_on`.** It falls due when the van has
driven far enough; an invented date beside it would only be ignored, and would
show as overdue on a screen that had no business saying so.

## Permission keys — extending `Assets`, not a new group

**No new keys.** The existing `Assets` group is enough:

| Action | Gate | Why |
|---|---|---|
| See the fleet | `assets.view` | Same screen family, same trust |
| Vehicle papers, plate, expiry dates | `assets.update` | Restating what a vehicle *is* |
| Log a trip, record a fill | `assets.transfer` | See below |
| Book / complete distance servicing | `assets.maintain` | Already the servicing gate |

I considered adding `assets.log-usage` and decided against it. Recording that a
van did 360 km and took 55 litres is the **same custodial job** as signing
equipment out to somebody — a storeman's work, exactly what `assets.transfer`
was carved out of `assets.update` to describe. A fifth key would draw a line
between "hand the driver the keys" and "write down what he did with them", which
is not a line any business draws.

A separate `Fleet` permission group would have been worse still: it implies a
separate module, and the whole argument of this phase is that fleet is not one.

**Consequence: no edit to `app/Support/Permissions.php` or
`database/seeders/RolePermissionSeeder.php` is required.**

## Wiring still to be done by the owners of those files

I did not touch `routes/*`, `config/*`, `app/Support/Guides.php` or
`app/Support/Permissions.php`. Three edits remain:

**1. `routes/web.php`** — beside the movements route:

```php
// Gated on `assets.view` for the same reason movements is: seeing the fleet
// comes before being trusted to log against it, and the buttons on the screen
// carry the stricter gates themselves.
Route::get('/assets/fleet', FleetVehicles::class)
    ->middleware('can:assets.view')->name('assets.fleet');
```

with `use App\Livewire\Fleet\Vehicles as FleetVehicles;`.

**2. `config/modules.php`** — extend the **existing** `assets` entry rather than
adding a `fleet` module. The models belong to the module that already owns
`FixedAsset`; a `fleet` module could be switched off independently and would
leave vehicle papers unreachable while the vans themselves stayed on the
register.

```php
'models' => [
    FixedAsset::class, AssetLocation::class, AssetTransfer::class,
    AssetMaintenance::class,
    VehicleDetail::class, VehicleTrip::class, FuelLog::class,
],
```

**3. Navigation** — a Fleet entry under Assets, gated on `assets.view`. Note
`resources/views/livewire/fleet/vehicles.blade.php` renders with
`'active' => 'assets'` so the Assets section stays highlighted.

A guide at `resources/guides/fleet.md` (registered in `app/Support/Guides.php`)
is the natural follow-up; `resources/guides/asset-movements.md` should gain a
line under **Work that repeats** noting that a vehicle may repeat on distance
instead of, or as well as, on months.
