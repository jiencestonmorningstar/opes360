### Nothing was duplicated

- **Evidence is a `BusinessDocument`.** `ComplianceRegister::attachEvidence()`
  delegates to `DocumentLinker::attach()` with role `supporting`, writing a
  `business_document_relations` row against the filing. There is no second
  document store, no file column, no upload path. Retention, legal hold,
  versioning, disposal and confidentiality therefore already apply to a filed
  tax receipt, unchanged, because it is an ordinary document.
  `test_evidence_is_an_ordinary_business_document` exists to keep it that way.
- **Sign-off is `WorkflowEngine`.** `ComplianceFiling` uses `Approvable` +
  `EmitsDomainEvents`; the module never touches steps, assignments or quorums.
  The listener reacts to the generic `workflow.approved` / `.rejected` /
  `.cancelled` / `.changes_requested` events, exactly as
  `SyncApprovedRequisitions` does.
- **Retention and legal hold were read, not rebuilt.** `DocumentRetention`
  already covers them and needs no compliance-specific variant: the retention
  period of a filed declaration is a property of the *document kind*, which is
  where the existing policy table already puts it.

## The design decisions worth challenging

### 1. A statutory deadline counts from the deadline, not from completion

This is the one place this module deliberately **does not** follow
`AssetServicing`, and it is the decision most worth arguing about.

An oil change six weeks late resets the engine's clock. A TVA return six weeks
late does **not** reset the tax calendar — the DGI sets the quarters, not the
business's punctuality. Counting from the completion date would push every
future deadline out by however late the last one was; after four late quarters
the register would be a year adrift from the law while looking perfectly
healthy.

But a licence renewed in March genuinely does run a year from March, because
that is what the new certificate says. One rule cannot serve both, so the
basis is a column: `compliance_obligations.schedule_basis` ∈ `due` |
`completion`, defaulting to `due`. Both cases are pinned by a test.

**Challenge it if:** you think a business would rather have one rule and a
manual date correction. The counter-argument is that the wrong rule is
invisible for one cycle and badly wrong by the fourth, which is precisely the
class of bug a compliance register must not have.

### 2. The obligation holds the next deadline; filings hold the past

Only one row anywhere answers "when is the next one" — `next_due_on` on the
obligation. Filings are history, and `due_on` is copied onto each one so
"was this late" survives the obligation rolling forward.

The tempting alternative — an open filing row that *is* the next deadline,
mirroring `asset_maintenance` — was rejected because it gives two rows the
same answer, and the day they disagree, the register is trusted and wrong.

### 3. Risk scores are computed, never stored

There is no `score` column. `inherentScore()` is `likelihood × impact`;
ranking is `orderByRaw('(likelihood * impact) desc')`. A stored score is a
third number that can contradict the two it came from, and it will, the first
time a screen updates one without recalculating.

### 4. The residual score is never derived from the controls attached

`residual_likelihood` / `residual_impact` are null until a person reassesses,
and `residualScore()` falls back to the inherent score until then. Attaching a
control lowers nothing. Automatically reducing the number when somebody types
a mitigation into a form produces a reassuring figure no human ever chose —
the single most dangerous thing a risk register can do.

### 5. Sign-off with no workflow defined is a refusal, not a wave-through

`ComplianceRegister::submit()` throws when `requires_approval` is true and
`Workflow::defaultFor(ComplianceFiling::class)` is null. Failing open would
mean the obligations somebody cared enough about to require sign-off on were
exactly the ones that skipped it. Same reasoning as the engine's own "a step
that resolves to nobody stalls".

### 6. Risk *reviews* count from when the review happened

The opposite of §1, and deliberately so. A review answers "how stale is what
we believe about this risk", and staleness runs from the last time somebody
actually looked. A review done three months late does buy another full
interval of confidence; a late tax return does not buy another quarter.

### 7. No `compliance.obligation.overdue` domain event

Overdue is a *state* the calendar computes, not a moment something happened.
An event for it would need a scheduler to fire it and would then fire again
every day the state stayed true. The calendar answers the question on demand.

## Permission keys to add

To `app/Support/Permissions.php::CATALOGUE`, and correspondingly to
`database/seeders/RolePermissionSeeder.php`:

```php
'Compliance' => ['view', 'manage', 'file'],
'Risks' => ['view', 'manage', 'review'],
```

Reasoning, key by key:

| Key | Who | Why it is separate |
|---|---|---|
| `compliance.view` | Manager and up | Seeing what the business owes the state is not sensitive; hiding it is how a deadline gets missed. Wide by default. |
| `compliance.manage` | Owner, Administrator | Defining an obligation means defining a cadence. Whoever can edit `interval_months` or set `is_active = false` can make a statutory duty disappear from the register — the compliance equivalent of writing yourself a workflow with no approver. Stops where `workflows.manage` stops, for the same reason. |
| `compliance.file` | Accountant, Manager | Recording that a return was filed. Separate from `manage` on the same principle as `sales.issue` vs. `sales.create`: an accountant files the TVA every quarter and should never be able to decide the TVA is no longer quarterly. |
| `risks.view` | Manager and up | |
| `risks.manage` | Owner, Administrator, Manager | Adding risks and controls. Wider than `compliance.manage` because identifying a risk is something you want people to do freely — a register only works if the people who see the problems can write them down. |
| `risks.review` | Owner, Administrator | **The one to argue about.** Reviewing sets the residual score, and the residual score is what decides whether a risk gets attention. Letting the risk's own owner mark their risk down is the conflict of interest the register exists to prevent. Held at Owner/Administrator on the same reasoning as `payroll.approve`. |

**There is deliberately no `compliance.approve`.** Approving a filing is the
workflow engine's business, and `docs/workflows.md` is explicit that being
asked *is* the permission. A second ability here would let the engine assign
an approver the gate then refuses.

## Registration still required (outside this phase's file ownership)

### 1. The listener — `app/Providers/AppServiceProvider.php`

One line, beside the existing four:

```php
Event::listen(DomainEvent::class, CompleteApprovedComplianceFilings::class);
```

**Until this line exists, an approved filing never completes and the deadline
never moves.** The tests register the listener explicitly with
`Event::listen(...)` and then drive a real approval through the real engine,
so the behaviour is proven — but the wiring is not, and cannot be from here.

### 2. `config/modules.php`

Placed after `assets`, before `banking`:

```php
'compliance' => [
    'label' => 'Compliance & risk',
    'description' => 'Statutory deadlines, the evidence they were met, and the risk register.',
    'icon' => 'shield-check',
    // On by default. A business that does not know it needs this is
    // precisely the business that needs it.
    'default' => true,
    'groups' => ['compliance', 'risks'],
    'models' => [
        ComplianceObligation::class,
        ComplianceFiling::class,
        Risk::class,
        RiskControl::class,
    ],
],
```

No `requires`. Compliance depends on documents for *evidence* but works
without any — an obligation with no attachment is still a deadline, and
requiring the Documents module would switch off the CNPS calendar of a
business that only wanted to turn off its letter templates.

### 3. Routes and screens — `routes/web.php`

Not built. The suggested shape, all reading `ComplianceCalendar`:

| Route | Screen | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| `/compliance` | Calendar: overdue, due soon, in progress, summary counters | `compliance.view` |
| `/compliance/obligations` | The register; create/edit an obligation | `compliance.manage` |
| `/compliance/filings/{filing}` | File it, attach evidence, submit for sign-off | `compliance.file` |
| `/risks` | Register, ranked by untreated severity | `risks.view` |
| `/risks/{risk}` | Controls, reassessment, review history | `risks.manage` |

Approvals need no new screen — `/actions` already lists a
`ComplianceFiling` waiting on you, because the engine's inbox is
subject-agnostic.

### 4. A default sign-off workflow

`requires_approval` obligations need a `Workflow` with
`subject_type = ComplianceFiling::class` and `is_default = true`. Workflows are
defined in data today (see `docs/workflows.md` — the workflow admin screen is
still missing), so this is a seeder or an admin task, not code.

## The read model, in one table

`App\Support\ComplianceCalendar` is the heart of the module. Everything else
is bookkeeping in service of it.

| Method | Answers |
|---|---|
| `overdue()` | Deadlines already missed |
| `dueSoon()` | Within each obligation's own `lead_days` — never a global number |
| `upcoming($days)` | Still ahead, excluding what was missed |
| `dueBetween($from, $to)` | Both ends **inclusive** |
| `inProgress()` | Filings started but not finished |
| `lateFilings($since)` | The evidence trail an inspector asks for |
| `risksDueForReview()` | Risks nobody has looked at since they promised to |
| `overdueControls()` | Mitigations everybody agreed on and nobody put in |
| `summary()` | Eight dashboard counters, computed once |

Overdue and due-soon are kept apart everywhere, on purpose: a list mixing the
deadlines you can still meet with the ones you cannot is a list people stop
opening, and an unopened register is the exact failure this module exists to
prevent.

## Hard-won lessons this phase actually hit

1. **`users.id` is BIGINT.** `foreignId` on `owner_id`, `created_by`,
   `completed_by`, `closed_by`; `foreignUlid` on everything else.
2. **64-char identifiers.** Every index and unique key is named explicitly.
   The longest auto-generated name in the migration is
   `compliance_filings_compliance_obligation_id_foreign` at 51 characters —
   inside the limit, but only just, which is why the composite index is
   `compliance_filings_company_obl_idx` rather than the name Laravel would
   have produced.
3. **`Model::create()` does not backfill DB defaults.** `status`, `is_active`,
   `treatment` and `identified_on` are set explicitly in the services.
4. **Attribute shadowing.** `ComplianceFiling::obligation()` is named against
   the `compliance_obligation_id` column, not `compliance_obligation()`.
5. **`Event::fake()` disables the listener under test.** The sign-off tests
   register the real listener and assert the real side effect — that
   `next_due_on` moved — rather than that an event was dispatched.
6. **Date casts and `whereBetween`.** `scopeDueBetween` widens to
   `startOfDay`/`endOfDay`. On a compliance calendar the dropped last day
   *is* the deadline, and
   `test_a_deadline_on_the_last_day_of_the_range_is_included` pins it.
7. **`BelongsToCompany`** on all four models, with a tenancy test each.

## Known gaps

- **No screens, routes or permission rows** — see above; file ownership.
- **No scheduled reminder.** The calendar answers on demand; nothing yet
  emails an owner that a deadline is a week away. This wants the same
  scheduled-job tier that document expiry alerts (§33) and e-signature
  reminders are still waiting on — one scheduler, not three.
- **No obligation ↔ risk link.** "We might miss the CNPS deadline" is a real
  risk about a real obligation, and a nullable `compliance_obligation_id` on
  `risks` would express it in one column. Left out to keep the two registers
  independently useful; add it when a screen actually wants it.
- **Retention of filing evidence is inherited, not configured.** A filed
  declaration takes the retention policy of its document *kind*. If a business
  needs "tax evidence for ten years" specifically, that is a
  `business_document_retention_policies` row against a tax document kind — a
  configuration task, not code.

## What shipped

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `app/Livewire/Compliance/Index.php` | The calendar. Overdue counted at the top and listed first |
| `resources/views/livewire/compliance/index.blade.php` | Tabs: what is due, obligations, filed |

Every state change goes through `ComplianceRegister` / `RiskRegister`, and every
`RuntimeException` they throw is surfaced with `addError` in the words the
service chose. The one exception is **defining** an obligation, which is written
to `ComplianceObligation::create()` because no service method exists for it —
the register's job starts at the first filing. Worth adding a
`ComplianceRegister::define()` if a second screen ever needs it.

Reassessing and reviewing a risk are gated on `risks.review`, never folded into
`risks.manage`, and there is a test that a manager is refused both.

The residual score is typed by a person. Adding a control flashes
"Control recorded. It does not change the score — a reassessment does."

render before the routes exist. Those guards can come out once the lines above

## Still not built

- **No evidence upload.** The filing form picks an existing `BusinessDocument`
  and links it through `attachEvidence()`. Uploading from here would be a
  second entry point into the document store; the document is created in
  Documents and pointed at from here.
- **No close/reopen on a risk.** `RiskRegister::close()` exists and demands a
  reason; no screen calls it yet.
- **No obligation editing.** Cadence and `is_active` can only be set at
  creation. Editing them is `compliance.manage` and wants its own screen — it
  is the action that can make a statutory duty disappear.
